\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chap:conclusion}

The collective goal of our contributions was to explore approaches that enable the separation of algorithm design from its performance optimization and the subsequent automation of this process via autotuning and LLM code generation.

We have introduced layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design approaches as practical solutions to enable user-directed optimization and performance portability in high-performance scientific applications, particularly those that are memory-bound. We have also demonstrated that these approaches simplify parallelization and distributed execution on CPU- and GPU-based systems. To enable even more seamless optimizations, we have then explored the potential of using LLMs to automatically generate optimized code for CUDA-enabled GPUs, and the practical effects of agnostic abstractions for LLM code generation.

Libraries Noarr and Cellato serve as ready-to-use prototype implementations of abstractions supporting layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design in \cpp for general-purpose scientific and cellular automata applications, respectively. They are open-source and available on GitHub, allowing for easy adoption in industry and research applications to enable user-directed and automated optimization. The libraries enable users to experiment with different combinations of layouts, traversal orders, and their transformations, which can be selected automatically using autotuning techniques.

To our knowledge, Noarr is the first library that provides a unified abstraction for layout-agnostic and traversal-agnostic algorithm design in \cpp. The current development of the language standard shows that such abstractions are becoming increasingly desirable, as demonstrated by the introduction of basic linear algebra algorithms in the \cpptwentysix standard, which shows some features of layout-agnostic design. The two libraries have been evaluated across a variety of use cases in their respective domains, demonstrating that they do not introduce significant overhead compared to hand-optimized implementations while providing substantial flexibility in changing data layouts and traversal orders without modifying the core algorithmic logic. Furthermore, Cellato provides domain-specific optimizations for cellular automata applications that can be applied automatically.

Our findings in the area of LLM-driven code optimization show that LLMs can benefit from user-directed guidance (tutoring) for specific optimization goals or design choices when optimization requires decisions that the model does not reliably infer from general prompts. At the same time, our findings suggest that contemporary abstractions for user-directed optimization, including other contemporary approaches promoting some level of layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design in \cpp, do not significantly benefit LLMs and, in many cases, hinder their ability to generate optimized code.

Open questions remain regarding sparse data structures in layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design and its adaptation to other specialized domains, such as graph algorithms. Emerging \cpp features, such as reflection, may enable even more powerful and flexible abstractions for algorithm design, which can draw from the experience gained in this work.

In the area of LLMs, further research may explore whether our findings generalize to newer, agentic LLM systems, which can interact with external tools and receive feedback on their work, enabling them to iteratively improve outputs.

The findings of this thesis should encourage further adoption of layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design approaches in scientific applications, as well as further research into their potential benefits and limitations. We hope that our work will contribute to developing more flexible and portable high-performance applications in a broader range of domains and to advancing the use of LLMs for code generation in scientific computing.
