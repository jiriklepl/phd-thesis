\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chap:conclusion}

We have introduced layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design approaches as practical solutions for enabling user-directed optimization and performance portability in high-performance scientific applications, particularly those that are memory-bound. We have also demonstrated that these approaches simplify parallelization and distributed execution on CPU- and GPU-based systems.

Noarr and Cellato serve as proof-of-concept implementations of abstractions supporting layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design in \cpp for general-purpose scientific and cellular automata applications, respectively. Both are implemented as header-only libraries, making them easy to integrate into existing applications. They are open-source and available on GitHub.

The two libraries have been evaluated across a variety of use cases in their respective domains, demonstrating that they do not introduce significant overhead compared to hand-optimized implementations while providing substantial flexibility in changing data layouts and traversal orders without modifying the core algorithm logic. Furthermore, Cellato provides domain-specific optimizations for cellular automata applications.

We have also explored the potential of using LLMs to automatically generate optimized code. Our findings show that LLMs can benefit from user-directed guidance (tutoring) for specific optimization goals or design choices when optimization requires decisions that the model does not reliably infer from general prompts. At the same time, our findings suggest that contemporary abstractions for user-directed optimization, including those promoting some level of layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design in \cpp, do not significantly benefit LLMs and, in many cases, hinder their ability to generate optimized code.

Open questions remain regarding sparse data structures in layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design, as well as other specialized domains such as graph algorithms. Emerging \cpp features, such as reflection, may enable even more powerful and flexible abstractions for layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design. In the area of LLMs, further research may explore whether our findings generalize to newer agentic LLM systems, which can interact with tools and receive feedback on their code generation, enabling them to iteratively improve their outputs.

% Overall, our work demonstrates feasibility and benefits of layout-agnostic and traversal-agnostic algorithm design in high-performance scientific applications
The findings of this thesis should encourage further adoption of layout- and traversal-agnostic algorithm design approaches in scientific applications, as well as further research into their potential benefits and limitations. We hope that our work will contribute to the development of more flexible and portable high-performance applications in a broader range of domains and to advancing the use of LLMs for code generation in scientific computing.
